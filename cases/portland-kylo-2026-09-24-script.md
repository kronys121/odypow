# Script: "90 Seconds in Portland" (Eddie Campbell / K9 Kylo, 2026-09-24)

Draft v1, 30.10.2026. Based on `portland-kylo-2026-09-24.md`.
Target runtime: 14-16 min (narration about 2,100 words at ~150 wpm, plus footage beats).

**Notation**
- `VO:` narration
- `[BWC]` PPB body-worn camera video (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sSYGuoBBMLk). Timecodes are TBD: assign them after watching the footage.
- `[AERIAL]` aerial footage, only if it is in the PPB video
- `[DOC]` on-screen excerpt from the DA memo (PDF), quote highlighted
- `[MAP]` animated map or parking-lot diagram (own graphics)
- `[STILL]` photo, with credit on screen
- `[SFX]` sound design

**Hard rules for the edit**
- Never show the moment of the shots, Campbell's body, or Kylo wounded. Cut to black or a diagram before the shots. Keep audio low or muted for the gunfire.
- Remove or heavily duck the audio after the shots, including the shout.
- Never play more than ~20-30 s of BWC without narration or on-screen analysis over it (fair use: commentary).
- Campbell is always "the suspect" or "Campbell" in connection with the Thompson killing. Never "the killer".

## COLD OPEN (0:00-1:00)

`[STILL]` Kylo, PPB photo. Slow push-in.

VO: On the morning of September 24th, 2026, a German shepherd named Kylo started what was supposed to be his last shift. After about six years with the Portland Police Bureau and 139 captures, he was set to retire.

`[BWC]` Officers on foot, before the parking lot. No shots.

VO: By two o'clock that afternoon, Kylo and the man he was chasing would both be dead. The whole thing, from the first shout to the last shot, took about ninety seconds.

`[SFX]` Hit to black.

VO: And when detectives picked up the gun that started it all, they found something nobody in that parking lot could have known.

`[TITLE CARD]` 90 SECONDS IN PORTLAND

---

## ACT 1. A WELFARE CHECK IN GRESHAM (1:00-3:00)

`[MAP]` Portland metro, zoom to Gresham, 17900 block of SE Market Street.

VO: Gresham, Oregon, a city on the eastern edge of the Portland metro area. Around seven in the morning, police are sent to a house on Southeast Market Street for a welfare check.

VO: Inside, they find the man who lives there: sixty-year-old Jeff Thompson. He's alone, and he's dead. He appears to have been shot in the head.

`[STILL]` Exterior street view or generic establishing shot of the area (no victim photo unless released by family or police).

VO: A multi-agency homicide unit, the East County Major Crimes Team, takes the case. Two things stand out almost immediately.

VO: First, Thompson's car is missing from the driveway.

VO: Second, a witness tells detectives that the night before, someone was seen at the house, talking with Thompson, and then going inside with him. Surveillance video shows Thompson's car leaving the area a short time later.

`[DOC]` "detectives observed Mr. Thompson's missing vehicle leaving the area on surveillance video."

VO: Detectives now have a car, a description, and a man who may have been the last person to see Jeff Thompson alive.

---

## ACT 2. THE HUNT (3:00-5:30)

`[GRAPHIC]` License plate reader animation.

VO: Detectives enter Thompson's license plate into the automatic license plate reader system as a "plate of interest." Those cameras are mounted on poles, on patrol cars, and in this case, on a private commercial vehicle.

VO: That vehicle drives past the plate. The alert puts Thompson's car near the Montavilla Motel, at Southeast 99th and Pine, in Portland.

`[MAP]` Gresham to Montavilla Motel, line animation.

VO: Detectives find the car parked on the street. They watch a man come and go from it. He matches the clothing and description the witness gave.

VO: Here the case takes a strange turn. Going through records, detectives believe they know who this man is: someone with a recent connection to Thompson and an open warrant on a firearms charge.

VO: They're wrong about the name. The man is actually thirty-nine-year-old Eddie Brandon Todd Campbell. But by coincidence, Campbell has a warrant too: a violation of his post-prison supervision on a weapons offense.

`[DOC]` "a different person than detectives initially thought, but who also had an outstanding warrant for his arrest"

VO: That warrant gives police a legal reason to pick him up and question him about the murder. The job goes to the U.S. Marshals Service Pacific Northwest Violent Offender Task Force: federal marshals working alongside local officers, including Portland Police. Their job is tracking down high-risk fugitives.

VO: Among them is Portland Police K9 officer Jerry Ables, a twenty-one-year veteran. And his partner, Kylo.

---

## ACT 3. NINETY SECONDS (5:30-10:00)

`[MAP]` Animated street map: Montavilla Motel, SE Pine St, SE 97th Ave, Chestnut Tree Inn. Use this map as the spine of the whole act.

VO: At about 1:48 in the afternoon, task force members pull up in two vehicles on the north side of the Montavilla Motel. They make contact with Campbell.

VO: He runs almost immediately: west on Pine, across 99th. Two people on the street later tell investigators they saw something silver in his hand. One of them thinks it's a gun.

`[AERIAL]` If available: Campbell running, the car on Pine.

VO: A law enforcement plane is overhead, and it records what happens next. Near 97th, a woman is driving east on Pine. Campbell runs up to her car and tries to take it.

VO: She refuses to get out. Instead, she hits the gas. Campbell ends up in the driver's seat with her as the car speeds east.

VO: Then Officer Ables's vehicle turns onto Pine, heading right at them, and blocks the road. The car slows. Campbell jumps out, runs alongside it for a few steps, then turns back and sprints west again.

`[PAUSE / BEAT]` 1-2 s of silence over the map.

VO: Think about that driver for a moment. She didn't get out, and that decision may have kept her out of everything that came next.

`[BWC]` Ables's camera: the stop and the K9 warning, if audible.

VO: At 97th, Ables stops and shouts a warning: stop running, or I'm sending the dog. Campbell keeps going. Ables releases Kylo and follows on foot.

VO: Campbell turns south on 97th and cuts into the parking lot of the Chestnut Tree Inn, on Southeast Stark Street. Kylo is right behind him.

`[BWC]` Fender's camera: exiting the vehicle, commands.

VO: Coming the other way up 97th is a second task force member: Portland Police Officer Kameron Fender, seventeen years on the job. He sees Campbell and the dog in the lot, gets out, and starts shouting commands.

VO: Kylo catches him. Investigators later conclude the dog most likely grabbed Campbell's backpack, not his body. There were no obvious bite marks at the autopsy. Surveillance video shows Kylo latching onto the backpack and dragging Campbell to the ground.

`[DIAGRAM]` Top-down parking lot: Campbell on the ground, Kylo behind him, Fender to the south about 21 ft, Ables to the east about 15 ft, the building behind. Two more figures approaching from 97th, 35-40 ft.

VO: Look at where everyone is. Campbell is on the ground, back against the building. Kylo is behind him, still holding on. Fender is in front of him. Ables is off to the side. The memo describes it as a triangle that boxes Campbell in. Neither officer has any cover. Two more task force members, a Clackamas County deputy and a deputy U.S. Marshal, are moving in from the street with rifles.

`[BWC]` Up to, but NOT including, the shots. Freeze frame, or cut to the diagram, the moment the gun is visible.

VO: And this is where it all turns. On both officers' body cameras, a silver handgun appears in Campbell's right hand.

`[DOC]` "He then holds it with both hands appearing to point it directly in Officer Fender's direction before transferring it to his left hand."

VO: Both officers order him to drop the gun. A man sitting in his car at the other end of the lot hears it too. Campbell doesn't drop it.

`[SFX]` Cut to black. Gunfire audio muted or reduced to a single low thud.

VO: Four officers fire, all at almost the same instant. Ables fires four times. Fender, five. The Clackamas deputy, three. The marshal, once. Thirteen rounds. About ten of them hit Campbell.

VO: Kylo, still holding the backpack, is directly behind him.

`[HOLD ON BLACK]` 2 s.

VO: According to reporting on the footage, after the shots Officer Ables can be heard shouting one word: "No."

---

## ACT 4. AFTER THE SHOTS (10:00-12:30)

`[BWC]` Post-incident only if no graphic content is visible. Otherwise the diagram.

VO: Task force members move in, kick the gun away and handcuff Campbell. Within a minute, patrol officers arrive and start CPR and first aid. Paramedics are on scene in about five minutes. At around 1:59 p.m., Eddie Campbell is pronounced dead.

`[STILL]` Kylo, procession photo (PPB social media, credited).

VO: Kylo is rushed to one animal hospital, then a second. Veterinarians recommend euthanasia. That afternoon, officers line up outside to salute him, and his body, draped in an American flag, is escorted to the Oregon Humane Society. It's the first Portland police dog killed in the line of duty since 2014. It was his last shift.

VO: In the days that follow, the bureau confirms what many had already guessed. Kylo was hit by police gunfire.

`[TRANSITION]` Evidence-board style.

VO: Then detectives look at Campbell's gun. It's a .22-caliber Jennings, a small, cheap semi-automatic pistol.

VO: There's no magazine in it. The chamber is empty.

`[DOC]` "found the magazine missing and the chamber of the firearm empty"

VO: The magazine turns up on the sidewalk along 97th, the street Campbell ran down seconds before the parking lot. It likely fell out during the chase. One live round is lying on the ground next to him. More are loose in his pants pocket.

VO: In other words, the gun pointed at Officer Fender could not have fired. But nobody in that parking lot knew that, and under the law, that's the question that matters: what the officers could reasonably believe in that moment, not what was found afterwards.

VO: Investigators also run the gun through the national ballistics network. The ATF compares it to shell casings recovered at Jeff Thompson's house in Gresham and reports what it calls a "presumptive significant link." The state crime lab still has to confirm it.

VO: Preliminary toxicology finds THC, methamphetamine and alcohol in Campbell's system.

---

## ACT 5. THE DECISION AND THE FIGHT OVER THE VIDEO (12:30-14:30)

`[DOC]` DA memo header.

VO: On October 8th, two weeks after the shooting, the Multnomah County District Attorney's Office, led by District Attorney Nathan Vasquez, announces its decision. The use of deadly force by all four officers was justified under Oregon law. No charges.

VO: The office doesn't even take the case to a grand jury. With multiple body cameras, aerial video, surveillance footage and independent witnesses, it says there are no material facts in dispute.

VO: The memo also addresses the hardest question head-on: officers fired knowing a police dog was right behind the suspect.

`[DOC]` "This remains true even with Kylo being in danger of being hit, as they had no other reasonably viable alternatives available to them given the immediate deadly threat Mr. Campbell presented."

VO: The same day, the Portland Police Bureau publishes its video. And that's where a second argument begins.

`[BWC]` Show the PPB video's title cards and narration framing (this is the point being discussed, so showing it is commentary).

VO: The bureau's release is an edited video summary with narration and context. Unedited portions of the footage are included in it. City Councilor Sameer Kanal objected publicly.

`[QUOTE CARD]` "Incomplete footage which goes beyond objective documentation of an incident undermines trust in both local law enforcement and the City broadly." (Sameer Kanal, via KATU)

VO: Kanal called for a clear written policy on how use-of-force footage is presented to the public.

VO: Police Chief Bob Day pushed back. Releasing raw video without context, he argued, can leave viewers "without important information needed to understand what they are seeing." The bureau says it releases both, so the public can choose.

VO: It's a debate that goes well beyond Portland. When police release their own footage of their own shootings, who decides what the public sees first?

---

## OUTRO (14:30-15:30)

`[STILL]` Chestnut Tree Inn parking lot, daytime, empty (own footage or licensed stock).

VO: Officers Ables and Fender were placed on standard administrative leave. The shooting still goes to an internal review, the Police Review Board, and a final ruling from the chief on whether it followed bureau policy. The state lab confirmation on the gun is still pending. And because Eddie Campbell is dead, the question of whether he killed Jeff Thompson will never be tested in a courtroom.

VO: What's left is ninety seconds of video, an empty gun, and a dog who was one shift away from retirement.

VO: Every document we used is linked in the description, including the full DA memo and the bureau's original footage. Read them, and tell us in the comments: should police be the ones editing the video of their own shootings?

`[END CARD]`
